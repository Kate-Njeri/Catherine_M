The Challenge

Client onboarding often looks simple from the outside: a client signs, information is collected, the team prepares, and work begins. But between “client signed” and “ready to deliver,” several small tasks have to happen in the right order.

Information needs to be collected, documents organized, internal tasks created, the team notified, and the client told what happens next. When these steps depend on someone remembering what to do next, onboarding can become inconsistent and difficult to track.

The goal was to create a clearer workflow that moved a new client from signed agreement to delivery readiness with fewer manual handoffs.

My Approach

1

Workflow Audit

Reviewed the onboarding journey from signed client through delivery readiness to identify the steps, information, tools, owners, and handoffs involved.

2

Workflow Mapping

Mapped the sequence of actions and identified where information was being collected, transferred, or recreated across the process.

3

Automation Opportunities

Separated the workflow into tasks that required human judgment and repetitive actions that could be triggered automatically.

4

Automation Build

Built the automated workflow in Zapier, connecting the relevant forms, email, project management, and notification steps.

5

Testing & Visibility

Tested the workflow across different onboarding scenarios and created clear status points so it was easier to see what had been completed and what still required action.

What I Built

  • Client onboarding trigger - starts the workflow when a new client enters the onboarding process.
  • Information collection - captures the information required before delivery begins.
  • Automated follow-up - sends the appropriate onboarding communication without requiring someone to remember each step.
  • Team notifications - alerts the relevant people when an onboarding milestone is reached.

Operational Impact

The workflow creates a more consistent path between signing a client and starting delivery. It reduces the number of routine handoffs that depend on memory, makes onboarding status easier to track, and gives the team a repeatable process they can use for every new client.

Lessons Learned

Automation works best when it follows a well-understood workflow.
The important part of this project wasn't starting with Zapier and asking, “What can I automate?”
It was starting with the workflow and asking, “What needs to happen between a signed client and the start of delivery?”
Once the process was clear, the automation opportunities became much easier to identify.
