The Challenge

As an executive assistant at a data company, I met a consultant who was helping the cofounder find clients on LinkedIn. The outreach was happening, but every prospect received the same generic message. Nobody tracked who had been contacted, who had accepted, or who was active on LinkedIn, and prospects who didn't reply were never followed up.

I was asked to help send the messages, but I could see that sending more of them through that process would only scale its gaps. The goal became making outreach consistent and visible: who had been contacted, who had accepted, which conversations were active, and what needed to happen next.

My Contribution

1

Built the Prospect Tracker

Created a tracker that recorded every prospect's status as they moved from connection request to accepted connection, message sent, reply, a move to email or phone, and a booked discovery call. It also noted whether each prospect was active on LinkedIn. I started it in Notion and moved it to Excel so the results could be analyzed more easily.

2

Ran the Weekly Outreach

Sent connection requests to the prospects the consultant had filtered, then messaged each one after they accepted. I increased the weekly volume from about 20 messages to 100, and kept each conversation going on LinkedIn until the prospect shared an email address or phone number.

3

Prepared Message and Follow-Up Drafts

Wrote the outreach messages and follow-up drafts so each stage had a ready, consistent message instead of the same generic note going to everyone.

4

Made Follow-Up Visible

Structured the tracker so it was always clear who had replied, who had gone quiet, and who needed a next step, so prospects who didn't reply were followed up instead of forgotten.

5

Documented the Workflow

Wrote the process up as an SOP covering the stages from first connection request to booked call, and handed it to the sales and marketing team to run on their own.

Results During the Tracking Period

  • Outreach volume increased: over the one month I tracked, weekly messages to people who had accepted a connection increased from about 20 to 100.
  • 254 responses received from that outreach, and 175 of them were qualified leads.
  • 3 discovery calls were booked that month.
  • The workflow was documented and handed over so the team had a repeatable reference for outreach and follow-up.

What I Learned

Volume wasn't the problem. Without tracking and clear follow-up, more messages would only have produced more noise and no way to see what was working, so the structure had to come before the scale. Documenting the workflow also meant the process no longer depended on individual memory and could be handed over cleanly. And I learned to let the tool follow the question: I started in Notion and moved to Excel once I needed to analyze the data.

How I Apply This Now

I set up tracking and a clear next step before scaling or automating anything, so it is always visible what is actually working. That is how I approach every workflow and automation I build for clients now: process first, then workflow, then automation.
